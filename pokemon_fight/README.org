#+TITLE: Pokemon Pocket Arena

* Play

A single-player TypeScript arena game for John and Luke. Choose Radiant Steelix,
Duskull, Greninja, or Darmanitan. Move with arrow keys / WASD on a computer or the
thumbstick on iPhone. Targeting, basic attacks, pickup collection, and charged
specials are automatic. The computer buddy is optional. All four are available
immediately, and each is designed to work without a buddy.

The first visit offers a safe movement practice. Pick an adventure, clear two
short waves, and challenge its leader. Three minutes is a pacing goal, not a
failure deadline. Enemy stripes and
circles warn of attacks. Stone shelters block water stars, tail waves, and enemy
projectiles. Ghost flames pass through cover and fireballs lob over it.
Stand close to a downed buddy to help; a living buddy can rescue the player once.

* Challenge levels

Choose Easy, Normal, Hard, or Expert before an adventure. Normal is the default.
All four settings work with every stage, hero, and optional computer buddy.
The current level appears beside the battle phase. Your choice is remembered.
Existing saves from the two-level version move to Normal once; selecting Easy
in the new menu keeps that choice on later visits. Badges and hero choices remain.

| Level  | Maximum enemies | Spawn interval | Shooter speed | Boss warning |
|--------+-----------------+----------------+---------------+--------------|
| Easy   |               5 | 3.40 seconds   |           125 | 1.9 seconds  |
| Normal |               8 | 2.20 seconds   |           155 | 1.5 seconds  |
| Hard   |              11 | 1.60 seconds   |           185 | 1.2 seconds  |
| Expert |              14 | 1.15 seconds   |           215 | 1.0 seconds  |

Speeds use arena units per second. Hard and Expert shooters fire three-shot fans.
Higher settings also increase pursuit speed, shorten charging windups and leader
rest periods, toughen enemies, and reduce healing supplies and hit protection.
Every setting retains visible attack warnings, automatic attacks, the safe
movement tutorial, and the same two upgrade breaks. Easy keeps the original
relaxed battle tuning. Normal adds action without adding controls.

* Five adventures

All five stages are available from the start. Each has its own map, scenery,
enemy mix, leader patterns, and optional bonus mission.

| Stage          | Leader          | Bonus mission                |
|----------------+-----------------+------------------------------|
| Meadow Camp    | Machamp         | Clear 12 challengers         |
| Moonlit Woods  | Duskull         | Help two lost friends        |
| Crystal Quarry | Radiant Steelix | Gather four crystals         |
| Ember Ruins    | Darmanitan      | Light three lanterns         |
| Sky Temple     | Greninja        | Charge two sky beacons       |

Walk onto crystals to collect them. Stand near friends or lanterns for a short
moment, or remain by a sky beacon for two seconds. A filling ring shows progress.
Completing a bonus restores two hearts and charges the special. The leader
arrives even if the bonus is unfinished, so children can finish each adventure
at their own pace. A mint pointer guides the player to an offscreen bonus.

Victory awards a stage badge and offers a Next adventure button. Cleared-stage
and bonus badges are saved locally when storage is available. Any stage can be
replayed or selected directly. A new stage starts with fresh health and powers;
the chosen Pokemon and buddy settings stay selected.

The woodland leader creates haunting wisps, the quarry leader opens ground
fissures, the ruins leader calls delayed eruptions, and the temple leader fires
water-star fans. Each has a marked second phase. The safe tutorial uses its own
simple obstacle layout before switching to the selected stage's map.

* Four ways to battle

| Pokemon           | Automatic basic attack                             |
|-------------------+----------------------------------------------------|
| Radiant Steelix   | A fan-shaped tail wave hits groups and pushes back |
| Duskull           | Tracking flames attach, haunt, and slow             |
| Greninja          | Three quick spinning water stars pierce a line     |
| Darmanitan        | Lobbed fireballs explode and leave flame patches   |

Each attack has its own shape, timing, sound, and collision behavior. Friendly
flame patches have mint-colored borders and never damage the player or buddy.
The original automatic specials remain available without another action button.

At about 28 and 58 seconds of match time, combat pauses completely for a power-up
choice. Tap one of three large cards, or press 1, 2, or 3 on a keyboard. Two
options develop the selected Pokemon's attack; one offers health, movement, or
energy help. Choices do not repeat within a round, and a new round starts fresh.
Examples include a second tail wave, twin spirits, returning water stars, and
longer-lasting fire patches. The buddy does not take the player's upgrades.

At both power-up checkpoints, the computer buddy levels up automatically. It
always chooses a non-repeating attack-related skill for its own Pokemon, with
no extra dialog or input and no consumption of the player's three choices:

| Buddy       | First attack upgrade | Second attack upgrade |
|-------------+----------------------+-----------------------|
| Steelix     | Double tail          | Wide sweep            |
| Duskull     | Twin spirits         | Long haunt            |
| Greninja    | Three ways           | Come back             |
| Darmanitan  | Double fire          | Big bloom             |

The player's upgrade panel reports the buddy's new skill. A downed buddy still
learns its upgrade and uses it after being helped back up. Both characters'
powers reset for the next stage while stage badge progress remains saved.

Chargers mark an orange stripe before dashing in a locked direction. Shielded
guards slowly turn their front shield: flank them, use haunting or fire, or break
the shield with Steelix's special. Machamp changes attack combinations at half
health, adding a cross punch and a slam at the player's previous position.

* Phone view and controls

Pause with the button, Escape, or P. Switching tabs or losing window focus also
pauses the game. Use Relaxed for a gentler first round. Landscape gives more room
on a phone; portrait is also supported. Settings are saved locally when available.

On phones and narrow windows, a close-up camera follows the player and looks
slightly toward the current target. The Pokemon's illustration is larger than in
the original whole-arena view. Offscreen portrait pointers help find enemies and
the buddy. Edge padding keeps the player's picture visible at the arena boundary.
The desktop view still shows the whole arena. The boss health bar sits outside
the arena rather than covering the Pokemon pictures. The phone selection screen uses
large, horizontally scrollable cards; swipe to choose a Pokemon. Camera zoom does
not change the character's game-world collision size, which the ground ring marks.

* Build and verify

Run from the repository root:

#+begin_src sh
npm run typecheck:pokemon_fight
npm run build:pokemon_fight
npm run test:pokemon_fight
node pokemon_fight/ts/build.mjs --check
python3 -m http.server 8000 --directory docs
#+end_src

Open http://localhost:8000/pokemon_fight/ in a computer browser. For an actual
iPhone, use the computer's reachable local-network address in place of localhost,
or the deployed GitHub Pages address after the normal repository publishing flow.
Opening index.html with file:// is not the supported way to run the game.

The repository home page links to the generated game. Changes to ts/ or web/
require rebuilding; docs/pokemon_fight/ is the generated, deployable output.
The build copies original photos unchanged and checks all emitted assets.

The app rotates and crops the original card illustrations at render time and
retains small canvases. It does not redraw the Pokemon or modify the source
photos. These are illustration badges, not transparent character cutouts. The
seven full-resolution source JPEGs total about 23 MB; they load sequentially,
and battle can start once the selected character is ready. Missing art falls
back to labeled color badges. Reducing download size is a future asset-production
improvement. Browser image caching controls subsequent downloads.

All combat values and signature mechanics are simplified game designs, not the
printed card rules. Steelix has a compact, visible ground hit circle, and Duskull
retains a visible picture during its shield. The first version has no human
multiplayer or networking; future cooperation is intended for the same device.

* Verification limits

Automated tests exercise the deterministic game simulation, including distinct
weapons, cover exceptions, lingering damage, power-up pauses and effects, guards,
charges, boss phases, partner rescue, and complete solo rounds. Phone camera
geometry checks cover both orientations and arena edges. Stage tests cover all
maps, leader patterns, reachable bonuses, isolated collision geometry, and
complete solo runs with every hero. Companion tests verify automatic offensive
choices and their projectile effects. The published-app test
runs real input handlers, upgrades, rendering calls, and audio profiles with DOM,
canvas, and audio stubs. These checks are
not a substitute for browser rendering, real iPhone touch/audio checks, or John
and Luke playtests. Actual device usability, performance, and final difficulty
still require those checks.
